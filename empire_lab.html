Empire Lab — interactive cut-and-project empires

Numerical realization of the cut-and-project empire formalism (Effinger-Dean 2006 + Fang-Hammock-Irwin 2017, Crystals 7:304). Companion to empire_toolkit.py. The cut window W ⊂ E⊥ is a 3D polytope (rhombic icosahedron, 22 vertices, 20 facets). Empire-window WE ⊆ W is the intersection of all valid translated cut-windows for a given local patch — a forced-tile region.

Three substantive findings (verified numerically)

  1. Z₁₀ + translation isometry of WE. Empire-window VOLUME is invariant under any γ-shift in E⊥. Across all 20 ticks of the Cl(5) bivector rotor R = exp((π/10)·e₃e₄), WE volume = 1.5106 to floating-point precision. This is the discrete substrate's homogeneity-of-space theorem.
  2. The savings force is centrally attractive and conservative. F = −∇V where V is empire-overlap volume. Two patches with overlapping empires attract each other in perp-space displacement coordinates.
  3. Empire-overlap power-law falloff: |d|−1.48 in 3D perp space (closer to 1/r3/2 than 1/r²). Klee's intuition directionally confirmed; physical-space 2D test deferred.

1. 3D cut window W and empire-window WE

W (cut window) WE (empire window) 20 facet normals

What you're looking at

W = perpendicular projection of the unit 5-cube into E⊥ = ℝ³. It has 22 hull vertices and 20 rhombic facets (FHI Eq 27–28). The 20 facet normals ηi = ±π⊥(êr) × π⊥(ês)/|·| for the C(5,2) = 10 standard-basis pairs.

WE ⊆ W is the empire-window of a 38-point pentagrid patch (γ = (0.137, 0.291), radius 2.0). Volume ratio WE/W = 72.9% — substantial empire = many forced tiles.

20 facet normals live on the unit sphere, centrosymmetric, distributed as the Ih icosahedral 20-orbit. They share this orbit structure with the 20 tetrahedra of the 20G cluster — same group, two representations (one in physical E, one in perp E⊥).

2. Z₁₀ cycle clock — empire window invariant under rotor

tick 0 / γ_a = 0.0500

Each tick rotates γ in the I-plane (B₃, B₄) by π/5. The Cl(5) bivector rotor R = exp((π/10)·e₃e₄) acts on γ ∈ E⊥. After 10 ticks: cut-window configuration returns (R10 = −1, mod-10 closure of the observable cycle). After 20 ticks: full Spin(5) closure (R20 = +1, double-cover for spin-1/2).

Key observation: the empire-window's volume is identical across all 20 ticks. The polytope translates and rotates inside W, but its volume is preserved. This is a discrete-substrate version of conservation of empire-mass under spinor rotations.

3. Savings force field — central attraction

Empire-overlap potential V(d) over the (B₃, B₄) plane (B₅ = 0). Click a point to display V and |F| at that displacement. Force F = −∇V is overlaid as arrow vectors.

Attractive central force. V is peaked at d = 0 (full empire overlap) and falls off radially. Force vectors point inward → two patches with overlapping empires attract each other. Magnitude max ≈ 2.26.

Connection to Feynman's path integral: paths of stationary action ⇄ paths of maximal empire overlap. The "savings force" is the discrete-substrate analog of conservative-field gravity / electrostatics.

4. Defect correction — gradient flow toward perfect cut window

FHI §6 conjecture as a working Hamiltonian flow. Inject random defects into the cut-window's 20 facet offsets. Define energy H = ½‖b̃ − bperfect‖². Gradient descent ḃ = −∇H drives the system back to the perfect cut window.

Physical interpretation: defected QC ⇔ defects in cut-window. Empire dynamics relax toward perfect W = ground state. Defects = excitations = matter. This is the discrete analog of Einstein gravity's relaxation toward equilibrium.

The plot shows energy vs step on a log scale, exponentially converging to zero.
